# Lynch-Syndrome 
# The Case of Patient X: Overcoming Lynch Syndrome & dMMR Cancer

A real-world clinical narrative exploring **DNA Mismatch Repair (MMR) deficiency**, Microsatellite Instability (MSI), and the revolutionary use of PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy (*Dostarlimab*).

## 📖 Introduction
Cells contain highly complex systems to ensure integrity of our human genome. DNA replication and Repair focuses on one of the most important systems in the cell: Mismatch Repair (MMR).

During DNA replication, the polymerase enzyme has the huge task of replicating billions of DNA codes. However this system is not perfect and can sometimes cause mistakes. This can be either the addition of incorrect bases (Mismatch) or when the polymerase slips in areas of high repetition of DNA (Insertion/Deletion loop). The MMR system is the repair system within cells. It follows behind the polymerase enzyme to identify and repair any mistakes.

Lynch Syndrome is the most common hereditary cancer predisposition syndrome. This is caused by a germline mutation of one of the key MMR genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2). People with Lynch Syndrome have one faulty MMR gene in every cell of their body. Cancer occurs through a two hit mechanism. One random mutation occurs in a somatic cell (such as the colon or rectum) which causes the second copy of the gene to become faulty.

This one cell now has no functional MMR proteins, known as Mismatch Repair Deficient (dMMR). This cell has a complete breakdown of the repair system. As a result it has extreme genomic instability. During cell division it will accumulate hundreds/thousands of errors. This is known as a mutator phenotype. Key marker of this is Microsatellite Instability High (MSI-H). This is when the repetitive DNA sequences (microsatellites) quickly change due to the polymerase not changing. The rapid increase in mutations in other key genes causes the cells to become cancerous.

Traditionally locally advanced rectal cancers are treated with a trimodal approach. This includes Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy and Radiotherapy then complex surgery to remove part of the rectum. However this is very morbid and can cause permanent colostomies, infertility and long term bowel, urinary and sexual problems. This case study describes a revolutionary treatment that uses the DNA repair defects of the tumour to eradicate it.
