# Lynch-Syndrome | The Case of Patient X: Overcoming Lynch Syndrome & dMMR Cancer

A real-world clinical narrative exploring **DNA Mismatch Repair (MMR) deficiency**, Microsatellite Instability (MSI), and the revolutionary use of PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy (*Dostarlimab*).

## 📖 Introduction

The cells contain an highly complex systems to ensure the integrity of our human genome. DNA replication and Repair focuses on one of the most important systems in the cell: Mismatch Repair (MMR).

During DNA replication, the polymerase enzyme has the main task of replicating billions of DNA codes. However this system is not perfect and can sometimes cause mistakes. This can be either the addition of incorrect bases (Mismatch) or when the polymerase slips in areas of high repetition of DNA (Insertion/Deletion loop). The MMR system is the repair system within cells. It follows behind the polymerase enzyme to identify and repair any mistakes.

Lynch Syndrome is the most common hereditary cancer predisposition syndrome. This is caused by a germline mutation of one of the key MMR genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2). People with Lynch Syndrome have one faulty MMR gene in every cell of their body. Cancer occurs through a two hit mechanism. One random mutation occurs in a somatic cell (such as the colon or rectum) which causes the second copy of the gene to become faulty.

This one cell now has no functional MMR proteins, known as Mismatch Repair Deficient (dMMR). This cell has a complete breakdown of the repair system. As a result it has extreme genomic instability. During cell division it will accumulate hundreds/thousands of errors. This is known as a mutator phenotype. Key marker of this is Microsatellite Instability High (MSI-H). This is when the repetitive DNA sequences (microsatellites) quickly change due to the polymerase not changing. The rapid increase in mutations in other highlighted genes causes the cells to become cancerous.

Traditionally locally advanced rectal cancers are treated with a trimodal approach. This includes Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy and Radiotherapy then complex surgery to remove part of the rectum. However this is very morbid and can cause permanent colostomies, infertility and long term bowel, urinary and sexual problems. This case study describes a revolutionary treatment that uses the DNA repair defects of the tumour to eradicate it.

## 📑 Case Presentation

Sascha Roth is a 38-year old female who was diagnosed with rectal adenocarcinoma stage II/III in 2022. As a known Lynch Syndrome carrier, this was an important detail to note. Her tumor biopsy was tested and proved to be MMR Deficient (dMMR).

She was offered the standard treatment of chemo, radiotherapy and life changing surgery by doctors at her local hospital in Washington DC. She was faced with a difficult choice to make as the impact of this would be severe and permanent.

Due to her tumor being dMMR, specialists at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center MSKCC offered her to be the first patient in a new phase 2 trial (NCT04165772). This trial led by gastrointestinal oncologists Dr Andrea Cercek and Dr Luis Diaz Jr was based on the radical theory that patients with locally advanced dMMR rectal cancers could be cured using immunotherapy only. This would mean that they wouldn’t have to undergo any standard toxic treatments. Sascha decided to join the trial.

## 💉 Treatment and Clinical Course

The treatment protocol was very simple. Sascha was given a drug called Dostarlimab (Jemperli) which is an immunotherapy, specifically a PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor. This drug was administered in controlled doses via intravenous infusion over a period of 3 weeks for 6 months.

She was not given any Chemotherapy, Radiotherapy or Surgery.

After completing the 6 month treatment Sascha attended follow-up appointments. These included Endoscopy, PET scans and MRI scans. Her doctors could find any evidence of cancer. The tumour had completely disappeared! This meant she had achieved a 100% complete clinical response.

This was not an isolated case. Every patient that joined the trial achieved the same result. As of June 2024, all 42 patients who have completed the treatment have achieved a 100% complete clinical response. None of them have experienced a relapse of cancer, some have been cancer free for up to 4 years. This amazing success has lead to the FDA granting Dostarlimab a "Breakthrough Therapy Designation" for the use of this drug.
